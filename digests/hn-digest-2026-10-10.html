Will we ever see a soundness bug in the lean kernel again?

To a software developer the question seems insane. There were bugs in the past, of course there will be more.

When we see those bugs, what will it mean for AI lean proofs? Can we trust them?

This whole thing boils down to trust.

We can trust human verifications highly because of community and reputation and human proof-of-work. Humans sometimes lie about math results but it's rare because of this. They make mistakes and those mistakes are discovered by communities who are themselves largely trustworthy because of this.

LLMs don't care about reputation. They hallucinate and fabricate often. In harnesses they literally try to cheat and bend rules, which is a disaster for knowledge that is encoded in rules. So we have to rely on proof checkers.

The problem is, how trustworthy are proof checkers? Are there bugs? And is the underlying theory itself free of paradoxes and unknowables and mathematical "bugs" that can be exploited? Imagine a human mathematician hell bent on deceiving other mathematicians - would we trust their breakthroughs, even with a verifier?

Because of these properties, the final backstop has to be humans, and rooted in the community and proof-of-work based human trust system. At the moment people are trusting the tools too much.

Prediction: bugs will be found by humans using AI tools that call into question the Navier-Stokes proof.
