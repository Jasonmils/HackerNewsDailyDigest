Homa: The end of TCP for AI clusters [video]

Homa：终结 AI 集群中的 TCP [视频]

▲ 65 分 · signa11 · 30 评论

斯坦福团队提出 Homa 传输协议，用接收方 GRANT 授权替代 TCP 的发送方拥塞控制，为 AI 集群低延迟 RPC 设计。但评论者批评其无整条消息丢失检测、无内置加密，基准测试每线程仅 4 Gbit/s，认为 QUIC 覆盖相近需求。

🧭 联系你的知识背景 AI 集群中节点频繁交换梯度与中间结果，类似分布式训练里的通信环节；传统 TCP 对每个小消息都维护连接和拥塞状态，Homa 改为由接收方按可接收内存发 GRANT 授权，减少小请求的往返延迟。

  • Homa 协议把消息分为立即发送的未调度部分和等待接收方 GRANT 的调度部分，以降低小请求延迟。
  • 反对者 Veserv 指出 Homa 无整条 RPC 丢失检测、无内置加密，60kB 消息每超线程仅 4 Gbit/s。
  • 支持者认为现代硬件交换机每包处理开销低，小包不再低效，但 Homa 不适合开放 Web。
  • QUIC 用流 ID 和流控解决相似问题，可能比 Homa 更干净，已被视为有力替代。

💬 讨论 HN 评论核心分歧在于 Homa 是接收方驱动的优雅方案，还是已被 QUIC 覆盖的过度设计；批评者给出每线程 4 Gbit/s 的低吞吐数据。

🔥 回复最多的评论 · Veserv · 3 回复

Homa is not a good design. [1]

1. No way to detect whole RPC loss. Since there is no outer connection state, if every packet in the send-side of a RPC is lost then there is no way for a server to detect that it should issue a resend. The RPC is just lost to the ether. This affects small messages, like messages that fit in a single packet, more since there are fewer packets in the send-side.

2. Related to the above, there is no builtin encryption support. So, if you want encryption then you need to layer it either above or below.

3. Benchmarked performance is awful. The 60 kB average message case in [2] Table 4 takes 5(!) hyperthreads to average 20 Gbit/s. That is just 4 Gbit/s per hyperthread. Even a totally naive one-packet per system call network protocol design and implementation should get to ~8 Gbit/s per hyperthread. 30 Gbit/s per hyperthread is easy with just a little focus on performance.

4. Despite all the performance design problems in QUIC (though still faster than Homa) it already solves basically every problem Homa is trying to solve in a much cleaner way. Stream IDs correspond to RPC IDs. Stream Max corresponds to Grants. Multiple streams under single Client allows prioritization.

Except you do not randomly lose entire messages. You can compact small messages into packets. You get more precise RTT time allowing more accurate pacing/congestion calculations. You get builtin encryption. It survives ossified middleboxs. It has multiple ack frames/packets reducing ack overhead.

The only real difference is that Homa uses explicit receiver Resend instead of implicit sender Resend. Except that actually consumes significantly more receiver resources in non-trivial loss scenarios especially due to Resend packets only supporting a single Resend span. It also incurs higher latency and has higher requirements on the entire lossy network path due to all the extra transiting data that you do not want to lose.

And those are just serious problems off the top of my head after reloading the RFC into my head. I can come up with some more if needed.

[2] https://www.usenix.org/system/files/atc21-ousterhout.pdf

How to scale intent, quality, and artistry with AI [video]

▲ 71 分 · simonjgreen · 22 评论
🔥 回复最多的评论 · socializer · 5 回复

The problem isn't that you can't use AI to uplevel your creativity. It's that doing it slows you down, so you end up at a marked disadvantage, because others who just prompt "hey Claude, make me X" will produce 100 good enough slop-pieces in the time it takes you to finish one.

For the vast majority of paying customers or social media appreciators, that low-effort slop is sufficient. It's cheap, fast, and gets better with every passing month. So if your overriding goal is to scale to win in that marketplace, then you can only scale by giving up on quality and artistry. It's a race to the bottom.

I think the only way to win is to push back on the notion that you need to work on superhuman scale in the first place. You can differentiate yourself from AI by asserting you're human and that there's inherent value in human art. You'll find some customers willing to pay extra for that, just like some hire a woodworker instead of buying at Ikea. But it's going to be a tough market for artisans because fewer and fewer people are forced to buy the fruits of your intellectual labor.

In software engineering specifically, we gleefully decided that speed and cost are the only thing that matters and we celebrate "hey Claude, make me X" projects the same as we celebrate human ingenuity (case in point: HN front page). So for SWEs, even that "I'm a human" route may be unavailable.

We're going to need default hard budget caps on pretty much everything

▲ 601 分 · elffjs · 299 评论
🔥 回复最多的评论 · motionlessveloc · 21 回复

I used to work on a support team of a well known backend type service that had hard budget caps.

It was, unfortunately, a nightmare. There were tons of tickets and even threats of lawsuits from customers whose service got cut off hard at the worst possible time due to organic growth/going viral/big event/nobody knew about the limit/etc. Not only did they lose all the leads and revenue they would have gotten from that bump, but they also pissed off their own existing users who suddenly couldn't use the service either.

Generally speaking, it's much better to use alerts instead of hard limit. Even in the worst case (hackers pwn your credentials and mine Bitcoin or whatever) the rest of your business is unaffected and you can negotiate with the billing department at comparative leisure.

This is all assuming you have humans operating the service. If you're letting AI agents yolo infra in prod, you have a whole series of new problems.
